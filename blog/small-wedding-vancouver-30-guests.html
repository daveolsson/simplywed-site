Planning a 30 person wedding in Vancouver

October 13, 2026
← All journal entries

Thirty guests is a real wedding. Not a compromise, not a "starter" version. It is a different kind of day, and it rewards a different kind of planning.

Most advice online is written for 120 people and then shrunk. That is backwards. Here is how to plan a small Vancouver wedding from the ground up, based on the ones we shoot.

Why 30 is a sweet spot

At 30, you can talk to everyone. Actually talk, not wave across a ballroom. Dinner is one long conversation. Speeches feel like toasts instead of performances.

It also changes your money. Per-guest costs go up a little, but the total drops hard. Fewer chairs, fewer centrepieces, less of everything. You can spend on the things you actually care about, like the food, the flowers, or the photographer.

Pick the room before the date

With a small guest list, the venue sets the whole mood. Skip the giant ballroom. A room built for 150 will swallow 30 people and feel empty, no matter how many candles you add.

Look for places built to be small. Private dining rooms. Restaurants you can book out. Heritage houses. Lofts with a single long table. Waterfront rooms with big windows. We have a full list in our guide to intimate wedding venues in Vancouver.

Two questions to ask every venue. What is the minimum spend for a small group? And is the room yours for the whole evening, or do you share it?

Keep the ceremony and reception close

If you can hold both in one place, do it. Moving 30 people is easy, but it still costs you an hour, and it costs your guests their energy.

Natural light does half your decor work. A room with big windows means fewer things to rent, fewer things to style, and better photos. Ask to see the room at the hour your ceremony will actually happen.

Where small weddings should spend

This is the contrarian part. Do not shrink everything evenly. Pick two or three things and go big on them.

Food is the obvious one. With 30 guests you can afford a proper multi-course dinner, or a chef who cooks for the table. Flowers are another. A wall of blooms behind the sweetheart table does more than a dozen small arrangements.

What can go? The head table, the extra favours, the DJ with a full light rig. A playlist and a good speaker handle 30 people fine.

A timeline that fits the day

A small wedding does not need a nine hour schedule. Here is a rough shape that works:

  1. Getting ready, 1 hour. Keep it quiet and close to the venue.
  2. Ceremony, 30 minutes. Short and personal beats long and formal.
  3. Portraits, 45 minutes. Do them before dinner if you can, so you rejoin the party fast.
  4. Dinner and toasts, 2 hours. This is the heart of the day.
  5. First dance and cake, 45 minutes. Then the night is yours.

That is about five to six hours door to door. It is also why a 5 hour photography package fits so many small weddings. Our 5 hour collection is $1,750, and our guide on how many hours of coverage you need walks through the math.

Photography for a small wedding

Here is the thing most couples miss. With fewer people, every guest is in the story. A good photographer on a 30 person day gets to know your people. We learn the names, the in-jokes, who is about to cry during the vows.

You also get a tighter gallery. No filler from a 200 person dance floor. Every frame is someone you love.

One honest tip: do not skip the family photos just because the group is small. Thirty people means you can do every combination in fifteen minutes. In ten years you will want them.

Mistakes we see

Inviting by guilt. A 30 person list works because it is tight. Every extra plus-one you add out of politeness changes the whole day. Decide your rule early and stick to it.

Over-planning the schedule. A small wedding should feel loose. If you have a 14 point run sheet, you have rebuilt the big wedding at small scale.

Forgetting the weather. Vancouver does rain. Pick a space with a real indoor option. Our post on rainy wedding days covers what to do about it.
