The best engagement photo locations around Vancouver

October 15, 2026
← All journal entries

Every "best engagement locations" list says the same ten places. Stanley Park. Gastown. A pretty beach. They are fine. They are also where three other couples are standing that Saturday.

Here is a better way to choose. Skip the list. Pick the feeling you want in your photos, then match a place to it.

If you want big and cinematic: the rocky coast

Whytecliff Park in West Vancouver is the one we keep going back to. Rock, water, and a small island sitting right off the shore. It looks like you travelled somewhere. You did not. It is a short drive from downtown.

Go near low tide if you can, and check the tide table before you commit. Wear shoes you can climb in. Rocks are uneven and nobody looks relaxed while wobbling.

If you want playful: anywhere with room to move

Rocks, open ground, a bit of slope. Movement is the cheat code for people who hate posing. Twirl, walk, race each other to the next log. The photos come out lively because you actually were.

If you want soft and golden: a field at the right hour

Open fields photograph best an hour before sunset. The light comes in low and warm, and everything glows. Summer works best for this. Spring is greener, fall is warmer in tone.

If you want moody: forest and rain

Vancouver's rainforest is the most underrated engagement setting we have. Moss, tall trees, soft grey light. Overcast days are the best days here, because the light is even and the colours deepen. Lynn Canyon and the trails around the North Shore are the usual starting points.

If you are a city couple: use the edges

You live downtown, you love it, and a forest would feel like a costume. Fine. Shoot the city, but pick its edges. Early morning streets before the crowds. The seawall at dusk. A rooftop if you have access. Avoid midday on a sunny Saturday, when every popular corner has a queue.

How to choose without overthinking

  1. Where do you actually spend time? The best photos happen somewhere you already feel at home.
  2. What do you want to feel in the photos? Epic, playful, warm, or quiet. Pick one word.
  3. What is the weather plan? Every location here needs a backup date or a different mood, not a cancelled shoot.
  4. How far will you walk? Some of the best views need a twenty minute hike. Say so before we plan it.

Timing matters more than the spot

A good place at a bad hour loses to a decent place at a good hour. For most seasons, book the last 90 minutes before sunset. Midday sun is harsh, and that is when parks fill up. If you can only do a lunchtime slot, we will pick shade.
